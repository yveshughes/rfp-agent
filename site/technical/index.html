THE END-TO-END DEMO

Billy does the work.
You make the call.

GLM 5.3 chooses the next action through Vultr Serverless Inference. Billy’s agent, browser, evidence, and saved work run on a Vultr VM.

  1. 01

    Build the company profile

    Attach a previous proposal, image or company file in chat, or ask Billy to review your website. He saves supported company facts with their sources.

  2. 02

    Find relevant opportunities

    Sources and the shared catalog feed company-specific matching. Billy considers capability evidence, geography and deadlines; the feed’s keyword score is only a preliminary signal.

  3. 03

    Start continuous Autopilot

    Billy picks one suitable RFP and adds it to My RFPs as Researching. He reads the source and original documents, identifies cited requirements and carries missing facts forward as explicit gaps.

  4. 04

    Prepare a response for review

    Three versioned response sections and an immutable PDF are saved. The first draft moves the RFP to Drafting; a current PDF moves it to Ready for review. Manual review checks remain yours.

  5. 05

    Keep the queue moving

    Billy immediately picks up the next suitable RFP while earlier responses await your review. Pause and Continue preserve his place. Individual source blockers are retained and skipped; no suitable matches or a wider blocker stops the queue.

  6. 06

    Review, then submit through the agency

    Click Ready for review to read and edit the full response in a continuous document canvas. Review and submit shows the PDF, deadline and flagged details. After your review, open the agency’s source and submission instructions. Billy does not submit, sign or email the proposal.

Implementation baseline: September 26, 2026. The real-model evidence-to-review-PDF path has been exercised; sequential queue behavior is also regression-tested with isolated model/source fixtures. A test fixture is not proof of universal portal coverage. No proposal was submitted.

WHY VULTR

A working agent with a home in the cloud.

Billy’s browser, company memory, and workflow run on Vultr. Your laptop is how you talk to him; his work runs on the server.

COMPUTE

Continuous opportunity monitoring

The VM checks watched sources and stores results between visits. A separately started Autopilot queue prepares responses sequentially; it does not automatically wake after exhausting current matches.

MEMORY

Company knowledge that compounds

Original PDFs, extracted evidence, company facts, and saved drafts live with the workflow. Document and website citations remain inspectable after refresh.

CONTROL

A business process with persistent state

One conversation can update a company fact and queue a follow-up. Saved changes, remaining gaps and preparation state remain visible across refreshes.

WHY NETBIRD

Private work. Controlled access.

Billy’s API never listens on a public port. The Vultr VM is a peer on a NetBird network; the owner’s Mac is another. Reviewers reach the workspace through NetBird’s authenticated reverse proxy over HTTPS, and the owner can also reach it directly over the encrypted peer-to-peer link. Proposals carry pricing and insurance, so the boundary comes first.

NETWORK

No inbound application port

The VM’s firewall admits port 8787 only on the NetBird interface. From the public internet the API does not answer; the public nginx site serves static pages only. The app additionally rejects any request whose host is not loopback or a configured NetBird hostname.

ACCESS

Authenticated reverse proxy

A NetBird Service publishes the peer’s port 8787 at an HTTPS address with automatic TLS. Login is a PIN or SSO at the proxy, before any request reaches the VM. HTTPS also makes voice calls possible, since browsers require a secure context for the microphone.

PEERS

Direct peer-to-peer

The owner’s Mac and the VM hold a WireGuard connection through NetBird. The Mac reaches Billy at the VM’s NetBird address with no SSH tunnel and no open port; measured round trip about 20 ms.

LIFECYCLE

Revoke from the dashboard

Disable the Service or remove a peer in NetBird and access ends. Approval to submit a proposal remains a separate, in-app human decision; NetBird governs who can reach the workspace, not what Billy may send.

Current access boundary

Two NetBird peers (VM and owner’s Mac), one authenticated HTTPS Service in front of the loopback-only API, firewall rule limited to the NetBird interface, SSH kept for administration only. There are still no per-user accounts inside the app; NetBird’s login is the gate. The public pitch on port 80 grants no access to the backend or company files.

What makes the walkthrough credible?

Establish fit first. Basic company capabilities must exist before Billy can explain a match. A previous response enriches that profile; it does not establish current insurance, pricing, or availability.

Use real requirements. The $1M Hartford versus $5M example illustrates gap handling. Only describe $5M as a Berkeley requirement if the selected RFP actually requires it.

Follow Berkeley’s submission method. The selected RFP requests email delivery. No delivery is performed by the current app; do not send an ESA-branded demonstration proposal to the city.

Show the evidence. Show the original source, a cited gap, a saved correction, and the generated PDF. Add a delivery receipt only after delivery is implemented and actually succeeds.

The private directory contains 6,222 source records; the opportunity feed combines watched-source discoveries and shared imports. Directory size is not a count of active RFPs or verified integrations. The full source list is excluded from the public repository.

Where is the app? Billy’s workspace is private. It runs on the Vultr VM behind a loopback API, reached over an authorized private connection, because proposals carry pricing and insurance details. Launch App forwards to that private connection, so it opens the workspace only on the presenter’s machine; it is shown live during judging.

05 / TECHNICAL Q&A

Decisions we can explain.

Current implementation decisions, reviewed against the September 26 continuous-Autopilot release. The public site and Billy’s private browser workspace run on Vultr. Main chat uses GLM 5.3 through Vultr Serverless Inference and generates review PDFs from saved sections. NetBird access and delivery are next.

What technologies run the application?

The backend uses Python, FastAPI, Uvicorn and SQLite. Playwright runs Chromium on the VM. pypdf extracts PDF text; ReportLab generates review PDFs; Poppler renders thumbnails; Pillow and Tesseract process image attachments. The workspace is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules with no frontend build step.

Vultr Serverless Inference hosts the configured model (deployment baseline: GLM 5.3). The VM hosts the agent and data, not model weights. nginx serves the public static site separately; systemd manages the private API.

How does continuous Autopilot work?

One selected RFP is prepared at a time. Picking it up makes it visible in My RFPs as Researching. Billy reads evidence, saves cited requirements and three draft sections, then exports a review PDF. Missing details remain placeholders.

The completed item stays ready for your review while Billy starts the next suitable match. Ready or already-handled items are excluded. A blocked source is retained with its reason and skipped; a blocker affecting the whole job, no matches, execution errors or the budget limit stops work. Pause and Continue retain progress. A service restart requires Continue; a completed queue does not automatically wake for new arrivals.

How are actions and usage bounded?

The model requests one validated tool at a time. Invalid action format allows up to three attempts; proposed completion is checked against the request and saved results. Normal turns allow 24 actions; Autopilot allows 80 per item, with one sequential queue task.

Token usage and conservative reservations are stored in a shared application budget ledger. The default limit is $100, configurable on the server. The fixed-rate estimator is an application safeguard, not current-price verification or an account-wide billing cap. Relevant company and document text is sent to the model provider; keys stay on the server.

No submission, email, purchasing, signing, shell or arbitrary HTTP tool is exposed.

What evidence can Billy use?

Company files can be attached in chat or added under Company Profile: PDF, PNG/JPEG/WebP, DOCX and UTF-8 text formats. Website facts retain source URLs and exact quotations. Original files remain intact; image OCR and text excerpts are labeled as such.

The server checks original-page quotations and requires reading all extracted pages of RFPs and reused documents before analysis/drafting. Extraction is bounded: at most 100 PDF pages per import, with original page ranges retained. A quotation establishes provenance, not current insurance, availability, prices or eligibility. Scanned PDF OCR is not implemented.

What does Ready for review mean?

A nonempty first section moves Researching to Drafting. Exporting the saved sections produces an immutable PDF and moves preparation to Ready for review. Editing a section makes the old PDF stale and returns the record to Drafting.

Clicking Ready for review opens a continuous editable response document. Save response writes changed sections with version protection; edits need a regenerated PDF. The review dialog shows the current PDF, deadline and unresolved requirements. Checklist percentages measure human review, not verified compliance. The renderer uses Times-Roman; exact agency formatting, signatures, forms, attachments and placeholders still need checking. The agency link does not submit or mark a bid Responded.

How do Sources and the shared catalog differ?

Sources is the directory and watched-source list. A scheduler checks every 15 seconds for sources due for approximately hourly review when browser ownership permits. There are tested listing adapters and conservative generic discovery; blocked or partial coverage is reported.

The shared catalog stores imported opportunities for all companies. Company-specific evidence drives preliminary ranking; private pursuits, notes and drafts stay local. Batch visibility can be reversed through an administrative API without deleting pursued work. Customer screens do not display ingestion names or batch controls.

How are matches prioritized?

The feed uses explainable lexical matching: 65% title-term overlap and 35% body-term overlap against company capabilities. It is not semantic search, an eligibility decision or a win probability.

Autopilot then evaluates fit, geography and time to prepare. Expired/closed records are rejected; among comparable feasible matches, nearer deadlines are favored. Unknown dates remain flagged, and precise closing times/timezones need source review. These model judgments still require evaluation.

How do company workspaces stay separate?

Each company has a separately bound runtime, SQLite database, original/generated files, browser session, chat and selected RFP. Background jobs retain their company when the UI switches. The directory, shared catalog and inference usage ledger are shared.

All companies run in one process for one trusted owner. Workspace IDs and write headers are not authentication or a multi-tenant security boundary. Use one Uvicorn worker; durable distributed execution is not implemented.

What is saved, and what survives a restart?

Company facts, source evidence, originals, drafts, review PDFs, messages, tool receipts and queue progress persist on the VM. Browser context and in-process tasks do not. Source watchers restart; interrupted agent work requires Continue.

SQLite and files must be backed up together. On-VM pre-change copies do not protect against VM loss. Scheduled off-VM backups, object storage, restore objectives and data-retention policy remain operational work.

What does Billy’s conversation and animation show?

Replies default to one or two concise sentences; detailed evidence stays in the profile and response workspace. Discussing lists topics to resolve. Activity shows current work and real document previews/page progress. Decisions holds explicit browser approvals.

Autopilot keeps a faster working animation and Autopilot label between steps. Idle/disconnected state uses a gray empty desk. Pause, reduced-motion and hidden-tab behavior are respected. Animation is a state indicator, not proof a tool succeeded.

Is voice connected?

Yes, as a phone call. Call Billy opens a full-duplex Gemini Live session with interruption. Gemini is only Billy’s ears and mouth: the server mints a single-use token with Billy’s instruction and two functions locked in, and every question about the company or his work is routed through ask_billy to the same GLM agent on Vultr that typed chat uses, so spoken answers are the agent’s answers and are saved in the chat.

Audio and replies pass through Google for that call. The Gemini key never leaves the server. Push-to-talk transcription through Meta remains optional.

How are browser and document operations protected?

Chromium runs as a non-root service with its sandbox enabled and a version-specific AppArmor allowance. Public network validation rejects private/reserved addresses and unusual schemes/ports. Source downloads revalidate redirects without browser credentials. WebSockets and service workers are disabled.

Human control prevents competing browser navigation. Forms and non-read requests have explicit approval handling, but GET side effects and custom portals are not universally classified. Hostile document extraction also needs stronger process isolation. Neither is a complete security-isolation claim.

How does a new engineer get started?

Start with the repository README and app/README.md. The versioned app/docs/ handoff covers capabilities, architecture, local configuration, data/API contracts, operations, recovery, tests and remaining work.

Use isolated local data and fake providers for workflow tests. The repository excludes credentials, private source data, company documents and historical deployment receipts. Git updates do not automatically deploy either the private checkout or the separate public static copy.
